Woojoo Na

A geometer, working across machine learning

I am a mathematician working on machine learning.

Trained in pure mathematics at Oxford, I take structures that already exist in geometry, algebraic geometry and topology, and use them to prove why neural networks and generative models behave the way they do: the right space, the shape of a set of solutions, the geometry of a path.

I am a PhD student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University, advised by Professor Jennifer Dy.

At Oxford, I did undergraduate research with Professor Christophe Petit, Professor Thomas Lukasiewicz, and Dr. Andrey Kormilitzin.

Research

My research answers foundational questions about neural networks and generative models with theorems, built from structures that already exist in geometry, algebraic geometry and topology. Selected papers are listed under each research direction below. A full list is on Google Scholar.

01Optimal transport on the space of covariances

Gaussian distributions with covariances A and B sit in a curved space: the Bures–Wasserstein geometry, where the distance is the optimal transport (W2) cost between them. I design monotone, descent-guaranteed updates on this geometry. In ITSPACE, proximal majorization–minimization on the exact Bures–Wasserstein objective gives closed-form updates in a square-root factorization, with sufficient decrease at every iteration in exact arithmetic and a certificate-gap bound when the polar computations are inexact.

  • ITSPACE: Monotone Gaussian Optimal Transport Updates Woojoo Na, Jennifer Dy ICML 2026 Sufficient decrease at every iteration, with closed-form updates that preserve positive semidefiniteness. OpenReviewarXiv

02Routing in mixture-of-experts language models

In a mixture-of-experts model, each token's route is a point in a simplex of expert patterns. When is a routing rule optimal, and how can every device stay balanced on every batch without retraining or dropping tokens? I study this in theory, through prices on groups of experts that move tokens between patterns, and on open MoE models with recorded user-conversation traffic.

  • Optimality and Load Balance of Group-Limited Routing in Mixture-of-Experts Woojoo Na, Jennifer Dy Under review, ICLR 2027 An LP-pricing router with joint rounding keeps every group within 2K−2 assignments of its target, with routing score at least the LP bound, no retraining and no dropped tokens.

03One-step generative modeling

Consistency models learn to jump from pure noise to data in a single step. With a fixed training budget, which noise levels should that budget be spent on? I relate the training noise-level distribution to the averaged loss and to the one-step generator's error, and show why a small loss can still leave a poor generator.

  • Small Loss, Poor Generator: Where Consistency Tuning Should Train for One-Step Generation Woojoo Na, Jennifer Dy Under review, ICLR 2027 A small loss does not certify the one-step generator. The resulting noise sampling improves Easy Consistency Tuning at small budgets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64).

Publications

  • ITSPACE: Monotone Gaussian Optimal Transport Updates Woojoo Na, Jennifer Dy International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), 2026 OpenReview arXiv
    Summary

    A proximal majorization–minimization method for the exact Bures–Wasserstein (Gaussian W2) objective on covariances, with closed-form updates in a square-root factorization. Each iteration is guaranteed to decrease the objective in exact arithmetic, with a certificate-gap bound under inexact polar computations; the updates preserve positive semidefiniteness and support rank-restricted factors. On real-world covariance-alignment benchmarks, ITSPACE reaches low-BW-gap solutions substantially faster than BW gradient descent and sample-OT baselines.

  • RACH-Space: Reconstructing Adaptive Convex Hull Space with Applications in Weak Supervision Woojoo Na, Abiy Tasissa arXiv preprint, 2023 arXiv
    Summary

    An algorithm for labeling unlabeled data in weakly supervised learning from incomplete, noisy information about the labels, based on reconstructing an adaptive convex hull. It reported state-of-the-art performance on the weak-supervision benchmark tasks considered at the time.

Earlier work

Undergraduate and early graduate research and projects, kept here for the record.

  • Trapdoor attacks on Cayley hash function parameters Alexander Allin, Woojoo Na, Christophe Petit · presented at IMACC 2019 A Cayley hash sends a bit string m1…mk to the product Am1···Amk in a finite group, a walk in its Cayley graph. Using the group structure, we gave a trapdoor attack on the parameters proposed at NutMiC 2019. Undergraduate research, Oxford Mathematical Institute.
  • p2-equivariant convolutional networks on MNIST Machine learning course project, 2022 A layer f is G-equivariant when f(g·x) = g·f(x) for every g in G. With G = p2, the wallpaper group of translations and half-turns, we studied group-equivariant CNNs, their uses and their limits, with experiments on MNIST.
  • Conceptual space of a transformer encoder Undergraduate summer research, Oxford Computer Science (Thomas Lukasiewicz), 2019 In conceptual-space theory a concept is a convex region of a space of qualities. We studied how transformer encoder word embeddings relate to this geometry.
  • Knowledge graph completion Undergraduate summer research, Oxford Computer Science (Thomas Lukasiewicz), 2018 A knowledge graph is a set of triples (h, r, t), edges labelled by relations. Completion predicts the missing triples; we studied it for ontology reasoning.
  • 2018 n2c2 challenge: adverse drug events and medication extraction Woojoo Na, Andrey Kormilitzin · undergraduate poster session, Oxford Mathematical Institute, 2018 · n2c2 Concept and relation extraction from clinical notes: label spans of text, then the arcs between them, with CNN and character-level word-embedding models.
  • Fake news detection and its impact on financial markets Brigade Commander Award, Republic of Korea Army start-up competition, 2021 An online platform that flags likely fake news with neural classifiers, aimed at small investors exposed to it.
